# Fee Disclosure Taxonomy & Source Map — Internal Draft

> **Status:** Private research draft; not published, not in the sitemap, and not an indexable page.
> **Last reviewed:** 2026-08-09
> **Evidence basis:** 33 privately verified public-source records; 25 records currently published in the Evidence Review.

## Purpose

Create a controlled vocabulary that helps consumers, journalists, researchers, and document-review workflows describe fee disclosures consistently. This draft is a classification framework, not a market survey and not evidence that any category is more common than another.

## Proposed classification dimensions

| Dimension | Controlled values or examples | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Fee status | mandatory, optional, conditional, prohibited-or-restricted-in-context, not stated | Separates a required charge from an add-on or a source-specific legal limitation |
| Disclosure timing | advertised price, quote/estimate, checkout, agreement/terms, statement/invoice, post-service, settlement | Identifies when a consumer is expected to see the charge |
| Authorization | explicit agreement, opt-in, optional selection, underlying agreement, government charge, not stated | Records what the source says about consent without inferring consent from a label |
| Fee purpose | processing/payment channel, administrative/document, maintenance, overdraft/returned item, add-on/product, medical/facility, renewal/termination, other | Normalizes different labels that may refer to related charge functions |
| Recurrence | one-time, recurring, event-triggered, unknown/not stated | Prevents a one-time fee from being treated as a subscription or continuing charge |
| Source document | advertisement, contract/terms, estimate, invoice/bill, statement, explanation of benefits, receipt, regulator guidance | Connects the category to where a consumer can verify it |
| Consumer action | request itemization, compare estimate and bill, inspect total price, opt in/opt out, dispute/complain, ask for written explanation, none stated | Converts classification into a practical next step when the source supports one |

## Candidate fee families

1. **Total-price and mandatory-charge disclosure** — upfront total, mandatory ancillary charge, government charge, shipping, optional add-on, final payment amount.
2. **Payment-channel and convenience fees** — pay-to-pay, online/phone payment, processing, card surcharge; keep legality limited to the cited source and jurisdiction.
3. **Bank-account processing fees** — overdraft, APSN/authorize-positive-settle-negative, NSF, returned-item, maintenance/account fee.
4. **Automotive transaction fees** — documentary/document, preparation, delivery, market adjustment, dealer-installed option, unwanted add-on, government/title/registration distinction.
5. **Medical estimate and billing disclosures** — good faith estimate, facility/hospital fee, itemized bill, explanation-of-benefits comparison, surprise bill, dispute fee.
6. **Subscription and contract lifecycle fees** — renewal, cancellation, early termination, notice window, automatic billing, amendment/change order. Current evidence coverage is insufficient for a public taxonomy section and requires additional primary sources.

## Evidence coverage decision

The current 33-record set is adequate for an internal schema and terminology map. It is not yet ready for a public taxonomy because coverage is uneven and the records are not a representative sample. The strongest supported initial release would focus on total-price disclosure, payment-channel fees, bank processing fees, automotive charges, and medical estimates/billing.

Before publication, add and verify primary-source coverage for:

- subscription renewal, cancellation, and termination disclosures;
- construction estimates, change orders, allowances, and scope exclusions;
- generic contract clauses involving administrative, termination, or escalation charges;
- invoice-specific fee terminology and reconciliation instructions; and
- at least one additional authoritative source for each major family where the current evidence is a single record.

These are coverage requirements, not invented sample-size claims. The publication review must decide whether the added sources justify a public taxonomy, a source map, or a narrower guide.

## Publication gate

Do not publish until the final asset has:

- a stated inclusion rule and collection period;
- a record-level source link for every term or classification;
- visible distinctions between source fact, DetectHiddenFees classification, interpretation, and limitation;
- a controlled-vocabulary change log;
- downloadable data only where the underlying public-source use is appropriate;
- a clear statement that record counts are reviewed-sample counts, not prevalence; and
- a useful consumer-facing explanation with links to the relevant existing DetectHiddenFees resources and contextual HiddenFeeAI tools.

## Current decision

**Keep private and continue evidence collection.** The taxonomy framework is publication-ready as a draft structure, but the public evidence base needs broader category coverage before release.
