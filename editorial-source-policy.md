# Editorial Source Policy

**Status:** Phase 3 governance draft | **Owner:** DetectHiddenFees Editorial Team

DetectHiddenFees uses sources to support factual claims, not to create the appearance of authority. A citation must support the nearby wording, scope, date, and jurisdiction. If a source cannot be checked, the claim is narrowed, labeled as an estimate or example, or withheld.

## Source hierarchy

1. Laws, regulations, court materials, and official government guidance.
2. Official regulators and consumer-protection agencies.
3. Government datasets.
4. Peer-reviewed academic research.
5. Standards bodies and official technical documentation.
6. First-party company documentation for that company’s own policies, prices, or capabilities.
7. Nonprofit and consumer-advocacy research with disclosed methods.
8. Reputable secondary journalism or industry analysis.
9. Carefully labeled secondary commentary.
10. User reports only as anecdotal experience, never as verified prevalence.

AI-generated text, search snippets, scraped pages, anonymous claims, and competitor marketing are not authoritative evidence.

## Applying sources

- A legal or regulatory statement must identify the jurisdiction, covered transaction or industry, effective date, and any material exceptions.
- Statistics must identify publisher, dataset, date range, sample or methodology, and limitations.
- Company pricing and product capabilities are cited to current first-party documentation and flagged for owner verification when repository evidence is absent.
- Estimates and examples are labeled as such; they are not presented as typical outcomes.
- Conflicting sources are described, dated, and resolved conservatively. The narrower defensible statement wins.
- Time-sensitive pages are reviewed at least annually, and sooner when laws, prices, product behavior, or safety guidance changes.
- Dead or materially changed sources are archived where legally appropriate or replaced with a primary source. The replacement is recorded in the page change log.
- Legal, financial, privacy, security, tax, and AI-performance claims require appropriate specialist review before publication when they go beyond general education.

## Citation standard

Use a readable inline citation or a Sources section. Include the institution, document title, publication/update date when available, direct URL, and access date for volatile pages. A source must not be used to imply that DetectHiddenFees independently verified a product claim unless it actually did.
