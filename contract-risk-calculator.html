What this contract risk calculator measures

A contract risk calculator is a triage tool. It helps you decide which agreement deserves a closer read before you spend time on every sentence. The score focuses on practical risk: can the price rise, can the agreement renew, can you exit, and do the terms shift control toward the provider?

Rate each signal from 0 to 1 based on what you see. Use 0 when the term is clear and balanced, .5 when you need more information, and 1 when the language is broad, uncapped, or difficult to exercise. Do not treat the score as a legal opinion; treat it as a reading priority.

Formula and methodology

The calculator uses weighted signals across cost, control, exit, dispute, and data terms. It is designed to make one cost, deadline, risk, or savings question visible without pretending to know facts that are not in the source document. Use the result as a documented estimate, save the assumptions, and compare it with the agreement, quote, bill, or invoice that created the obligation.

Write one evidence note beside every score. Quote the heading, record the page, and explain the practical consequence. That discipline keeps the score from becoming a vague feeling about the agreement. Review high-scoring terms together because they can compound: a renewal clause matters more when price can rise at renewal, and a termination fee matters more when the provider controls notice or service scope. The score should lead to questions, not panic.

Example and interpretation

An agreement with a clear price but a one-sided automatic renewal, uncapped escalation, and a large exit fee may feel inexpensive at signing while creating substantial future risk. The score surfaces that pattern even before you calculate the exact cost.

A higher score means “investigate before committing,” not “this contract is invalid.” A lower score is not a guarantee either. The most valuable follow-up is to connect each score to a section, dollar amount, deadline, or question for the other party.

Common mistakes to avoid

Pay special attention to terms that are easy to understand but hard to exercise: notice must be received, changes are effective on posting, cancellation requires a specific channel, or a dispute must be brought in a distant venue. Contract analysis should test the practical path, not just the sentence.

Frequently asked questions

What does the contract risk score measure?

It measures review priority across cost, renewal, exit, discretion, liability, disputes, and data terms. It is a screening model, not a legal risk score.

How should I score an unclear clause?

Use .5 when you need more information and 1 when the uncertainty could materially affect price, control, or exit. Keep a note explaining why.

Should I use this instead of a lawyer?

No. Use it to organize questions and decide where professional review may be warranted.
